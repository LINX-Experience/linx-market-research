## Psychology and Approach

**Archetype:** Innovator — Bruce Poon Tip explicitly said National Geographic Signature exists in a "luxury that doesn't exist in the market." He's repositioning G Adventures upmarket, not just launching a product.

**Key Motivation:** Breaking into the luxury market without abandoning the purpose-driven, community-first DNA. The Signature tier is designed to prove adventure travel and luxury are compatible.

**Top 3 Objections:**

1. "We're a mass-market brand — LINX is for ultra-luxury operators."
   Response: "LINX is for trust-based operators at the $5K+ per person threshold. The National Geographic Signature collection at ~$1,000/day is squarely in that range. Your Signature customer is not a G Adventures customer — they're asking trusted friends 'is this worth it?' LINX puts you in that conversation."

2. "We're just launching — not ready to add new distribution channels."
   Response: "Pre-launch is exactly when to build a trust graph. By the time first departures run Jan 2027, you want your Signature guests talking to their UHNW network. LINX builds that referral infrastructure before the first guest sets foot on safari."

3. "We already work with advisors and NatGeo's distribution."
   Response: "Advisors push products. LINX activates the guests who've been on the trip. After your first Signature departure, those 12 guests go home and their peer group asks what they did. LINX captures that referral and credits it back to you."

**Recommended Approach:** Pitch Bruce Poon Tip directly on the Signature tier as a peer-trust vehicle. Lead with: "You're entering a market where the buyer's first question is 'has anyone I trust done this?' LINX answers that question." Reference NatGeo Signature's purpose-driven positioning (learning, access, connection) as aligned with LINX's trust-verified community.

**Intro Path:** Cold outreach via G Adventures' media/trade press contacts, or via ILTM Cannes if G Adventures attends (not confirmed). No 1-degree connection found.
