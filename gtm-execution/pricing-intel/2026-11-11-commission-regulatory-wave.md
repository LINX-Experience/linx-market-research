## Signal 1: Air Canada Commission Cuts (Effective July 1, 2026)

**Source:** [Travelweek.ca](https://www.travelweek.ca/news/airlines/a-difficult-decision-but-a-necessary-step-air-canadas-new-commission-structure/) | ACTA response confirmed.

**What:** Air Canada implemented a reduced commission structure for travel advisors, effective July 1, 2026. Industry response: ACTA (Association of Canadian Travel Agencies) is "deeply concerned." Advisors describe the reduction as "outrageous."

**Pattern:** This follows years of airline commission compression. Delta, United, and most US carriers eliminated base commissions in the 2000s. Canada is now following the same trajectory.

**LINX implication:** Advisors earning less from airline commissions become more dependent on hotel/expedition/yacht commissions. When one revenue stream compresses, advisors guard the others more closely — but also become MORE receptive to alternative distribution partners who offer add-on earnings without requiring client reassignment. LINX's peer-referral model is compatible with advisor relationships (the advisor can still serve the client; the peer referrer earns only for the introduction).

## Signal 2: ASTA — Non-Commissionable Fares Reduce Advisor Income 20–30%

**Source:** [Travel Market Report](https://www.travelmarketreport.com/air/articles/asta-finds-that-non-commissionable-fares-reduce-advisor-commission-rates-by-as-much-as-30) | ASTA report, May 2026.

**What:** ASTA (American Society of Travel Advisors) published research finding that non-commissionable fares (NCFs) reduce actual advisor commission rates by 20% to 30% compared to stated rates. Airlines embed NCFs in complex fare structures, quietly reducing advisor income.

**LINX implication:** The effective commission rate for advisors is materially lower than the nominal rate. At 20–30% compression, an advisor nominally earning 15% on a hotel booking may actually net 10.5–12% after platform, host agency, and NCF adjustments. LINX's flat 10% peer-referral model — earned by a trusted peer who provides a qualified, high-intent referral — is now within range of an advisor's actual net earnings on many bookings. The economic case for peer referral closes as advisor net margins compress.

## Signal 3: NSW Australia — Mandatory Agent Commission Disclosure

**Source:** [Travel Weekly Australia](https://travelweekly.com.au/nsw-travel-agents-required-disclose-commissions-incentives-new-rules/)

**What:** New South Wales, Australia now requires travel agents to disclose all commissions and referral incentives that involve a financial benefit from a supplier. Agents must inform clients when their recommendation is financially incentivized.

**Geographic signal:** Australia typically leads regulatory trends that migrate to UK (via ATOL/ABTA), EU (via consumer protection directives), and eventually US. This is a leading indicator of the global direction.

**LINX implication:** "Incentivized recommendation" is what traditional advisor distribution IS — the agent recommends properties where they earn the highest commission, not necessarily the best fit. As disclosure requirements make this explicit to consumers, demand for authentically trust-based recommendations increases. LINX's peer-trust model is inherently disclosure-compatible:
- The referrer is a verified peer who has completed the experience
- The commission structure is transparent to all parties
- The recommendation is grounded in personal experience, not supplier incentive

Under disclosure regimes, LINX's model gains competitive advantage over incentivized advisor channels.
